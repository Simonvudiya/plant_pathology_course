Dickeya spp. (Bacterial Soft Rot / Blackleg)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
ClassGammaproteobacteria
OrderEnterobacterales
FamilyPectobacteriaceae
GenusDickeya
Key SpeciesD. solani, D. dadantii, D. dianthicola, D. chrysanthemi
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
Nutrient Agar (NA) / NAG Colonies appear as characteristic "fried egg" morphology — raised, cream-colored to pale yellow centres with irregular spreading margins[reference:0][reference:1]
MacConkey Agar Growth with lactose fermentation; colonies are pink-red[reference:2]
Crystal Violet Pectate (CVP) Forms pits (craters) in the medium due to pectinase activity; pink-red-orange cavities[reference:3]
Cell Morphology Gram-negative, rod-shaped, motile; oxidase and indole negative; facultative anaerobic[reference:4]
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample collection: Collect diseased stem or tuber tissue showing soft rot or blackleg symptoms. Surface-disinfect with 70% ethanol, then rinse with sterile distilled water.
  2. Maceration: Cut small fragments of affected tissue and macerate in sterile 0.85% NaCl solution[reference:5].
  3. Serial dilution: Prepare serial dilutions (10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁶) of the bacterial suspension.
  4. Plating: Streak onto Nutrient Agar (NA) or Nutrient Agar Glucose (NAG) plates. Alternatively, use CVP medium for selective isolation of pectinolytic species[reference:6].
  5. Incubation: Incubate at 25–28°C for 24–48 hours.
  6. Purification: Select single colonies showing "fried egg" morphology and restreak onto fresh NA plates for purity.
🔍 Identification Methods
  • Conventional PCR: Use pelADE and gapA gene primers to differentiate Dickeya from Pectobacterium. Expected amplicons: pelADE ~550 bp, gapA ~800 bp[reference:7].
  • Sequencing: Sequence the pelADE and gapA amplicons and compare with reference sequences (99%+ identity confirms species)[reference:8].
  • Biochemical tests: Oxidase negative, indole negative, catalase positive, able to use lactose as carbon source[reference:9].
  • Pathogenicity test: Inoculate healthy potato stems with a 10⁸ CFU/ml bacterial suspension by stabbing; observe water-soaked lesions and necrosis after 3–5 days at 28°C[reference:10].
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Blackleg (Potato)Stem necrosis starting at the base, black discoloration, wilting, and chlorotic leaves; stems may collapse.
Soft Rot (Tubers/Bulbs)Water-soaked, mushy rot of tuber tissue with a foul odor; tissue maceration and cavity formation.
Stem Rot (Maize)Soft rot of stem tissue, wilting, and lodging.
Hyacinth BulbsSoft rot of imported bulbs with characteristic "fried egg" colonies isolated on NAG medium[reference:11].
Zea mays (Maize) Irish potato Saintpaulia Hyacinthus

Ralstonia solanacearum (Bacterial Wilt)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
ClassBetaproteobacteria
OrderBurkholderiales
FamilyBurkholderiaceae
GenusRalstonia
Species ComplexR. solanacearum sensu lato (includes R. pseudosolanacearum, R. syzygii)
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
Modified SMSA Medium Virulent colonies: small, mucoid, irregular, whitish with a pink centre after 48–60 hours; blood-red whorls develop in the centre after further incubation[reference:12]
Tetrazolium Chloride (TZC) Medium Virulent colonies: creamy white, flat, irregular, fluidal with blood-red coloured whorls in the centre[reference:13][reference:14]
Nonvirulent Colonies Uniformly round, smaller, butyrous (dry), entirely red — distinguishable from virulent types on rich media[reference:15]
Cell Morphology Gram-negative, rod-shaped; slow-growing (colonies visible in less than 36 h at 28°C are not R. solanacearum)[reference:16]
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample collection: Collect diseased stem sections, vascular tissue, soil suspensions, or water samples[reference:17].
  2. Sample preparation: Surface-disinfect tissue, cut into small pieces, and macerate in sterile distilled water or 0.85% NaCl.
  3. Plating: Streak onto modified SMSA medium (semi-selective, contains antibiotics and fungicides to inhibit saprophytes) or TZC medium[reference:18].
  4. Incubation: Incubate at 28°C for 3–5 days. Examine plates at 48–60 hours for presumptive colonies.
  5. Purification: Select characteristic mucoid colonies with pink centres and restreak onto fresh medium for purity.
  6. Twitching motility test: Observe colonies at 18–24 h on solidified rich media for twitching motility — a distinctive colony morphology that helps distinguish R. solanacearum from other mucoid bacteria[reference:19].
Note: Other fast-growing bacterial species may form similar mucoid colonies; most appear red throughout. Do not rely solely on semi-selective medium for detection — complementary tests such as twitching motility or molecular methods are essential[reference:20].
🔍 Identification Methods
  • DAS-ELISA: Double antibody sandwich ELISA for high-throughput screening of soil and plant samples (used extensively in your dataset).
  • NCM ELISA: Nitrocellulose membrane ELISA for sensitive detection in potato tubers and soil.
  • Conventional PCR: Species-specific primer pairs (e.g., 759/760) targeting the 16S rRNA gene or hrpB gene; expected amplicon ~280 bp.
  • Phylotype-specific multiplex PCR: Differentiates phylotypes I, II, III, IV based on specific primer sets for epidemiological tracking.
  • Biovar identification: Biochemical tests based on carbon source utilization (e.g., disaccharides, hexose alcohols).
  • Pathogenicity test: Inoculate susceptible host plants and observe wilting symptoms; reisolate to fulfil Koch's postulates.
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Bacterial Wilt (Potato/Tomato)Sudden wilting of plants, vascular browning, and bacterial ooze streaming from cut stems placed in water.
Brown Rot (Potato Tubers)Vascular ring discoloration, brownish rot, and ooze from the stolon end of tubers.
Moko Disease (Banana)Wilting of leaves, vascular discoloration, and bacterial ooze from cut pseudostems.
Southern Wilt (Various)Stunting, yellowing, and wilting; vascular tissue shows brown discoloration.
Irish potato Tomato Banana Pepper Soil / Water

Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae (Bacterial Blight of Rice)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
ClassGammaproteobacteria
OrderLysobacterales (Xanthomonadales)
FamilyLysobacteraceae (Xanthomonadaceae)
GenusXanthomonas
Species / PathovarX. oryzae pv. oryzae (Xoo)
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
Nutrient Agar (NA) Raised, creamy yellow, smooth, entire, buttery colonies[reference:21]
Peptone Sucrose Agar (PSA) Colonies range from 0.08–1.1 mm, light yellow to yellow, with circular margins; majority are raised and slimy[reference:22]
Modified Wakimoto Medium Entire margins, smooth waxy texture; slow-growing characteristic colonies[reference:23]
Cell Morphology Gram-negative, rod-shaped, encapsulated, non-spore-forming[reference:24]
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample selection: Collect leaf tissue from the advancing margin of fresh bacterial blight lesions to maximise recovery of viable bacteria[reference:25].
  2. Surface sterilisation: Wash leaves, cut small segments from the lesion margin, and surface-disinfect briefly.
  3. Maceration: Macerate tissue in sterile distilled water or saline solution.
  4. Plating: Streak onto NA, PSA, or modified Wakimoto medium. Use semi-selective media to reduce saprophytic growth[reference:26].
  5. Incubation: Incubate at 25–28°C for 2–4 days (Xoo is slow-growing; colonies may take 3 days to reach 1–2 mm)[reference:27].
  6. Purification: Select single yellow colonies and restreak onto fresh medium for purity[reference:28].
🔍 Identification Methods
  • Conventional PCR: Use Xoo-specific primers targeting the 16S–23S rRNA ITS region or hrp gene clusters. Expected amplicons: ~470 bp (ITS), ~650 bp (hrp).
  • Real-time PCR (qPCR): TaqMan assays for sensitive detection and quantification in seed and leaf tissue.
  • Bio-PCR: Enrichment of viable cells on semi-selective medium followed by PCR for increased sensitivity.
  • ELISA: Serological detection using Xoo-specific antibodies for high-throughput screening[reference:29].
  • Biochemical tests: Oxidase negative, catalase positive, starch hydrolysis positive, nitrate reduction negative[reference:30].
  • Pathogenicity test: Clip-inoculate susceptible rice varieties and observe water-soaked lesions developing into yellow to white stripes[reference:31].
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Leaf Blight (Rice)Water-soaked lesions on leaf margins that turn yellow, then straw-white; lesions may coalesce to cover the entire leaf.
Kresek (Seedling Wilt)Wilting and death of young seedlings; leaves roll up and turn grey-green.
Bacterial OozeYellowish bacterial ooze droplets visible on lesion surfaces under humid conditions, especially in the morning.
Yield LossReduced tillering, grain filling, and up to 50–70% yield loss in severe infections.
Rice (Philippines) Rice (Burundi)

Agrobacterium tumefaciens (Crown Gall)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
ClassAlphaproteobacteria
OrderHyphomicrobiales (Rhizobiales)
FamilyRhizobiaceae
GenusAgrobacterium (syn. Rhizobium)
Speciestumefaciens (Smith & Townsend, 1907)
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
Yeast Mannitol Agar (YMA) White to cream-coloured, smooth, convex, glistening, circular with entire edges, mucoid colonies after 48–72 h[reference:32]
LB Medium Round, cream-coloured with pink tint, smooth edge, convex elevation[reference:33][reference:34]
AB Selection Medium Specific medium for A. tumefaciens selection; colonies show similar morphology (round, cream with pink tint)[reference:35]
Cell Morphology Gram-negative, rod-shaped, motile (peritrichous flagella), aerobic
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample collection: Collect gall tissue from the crown, roots, or stems of affected plants (e.g., rose, grapevine).
  2. Surface sterilisation: Wash galls thoroughly; surface-disinfect with 70% ethanol for 1 min followed by 10% bleach solution[reference:36].
  3. Tissue excision: Aseptically cut internal gall tissue into small fragments.
  4. Plating: Streak onto yeast mannitol agar (YMA), LB medium, or AB selection medium[reference:37].
  5. Incubation: Incubate at 25–28°C for 48–72 hours.
  6. Purification: Select single mucoid colonies and restreak for purity.
🔍 Identification Methods
  • Conventional PCR: Use virD2, virC, or ipt gene primers for Ti-plasmid detection; expected amplicons: ~224 bp (virD2), ~400 bp (ipt).
  • Koch's Postulate (Carrot Disc Test): Inoculate carrot slices with the isolate and observe crown gall tumour formation after 3 weeks[reference:38].
  • Biochemical tests: Growth on AB medium, 3-ketolactose production, oxidase positive, catalase positive.
  • Pathogenicity test: Inoculate susceptible host plants (e.g., tomato, sunflower) and observe gall development.
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Crown Gall (Rose, Grape)Rough, irregular, tumour-like galls at the crown (soil line), roots, or stems; galls may become woody and dark.
Stunted GrowthReduced vigour, yellowing, and wilting due to vascular disruption by galls.
Secondary InfectionsGalls provide entry points for secondary pathogens, leading to decay.
Rose (Netherlands, France, Ecuador) Grapevine Various dicotyledonous plants

Pectobacterium spp. (Soft Rot / Blackleg)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
ClassGammaproteobacteria
OrderEnterobacterales
FamilyPectobacteriaceae
GenusPectobacterium
Key SpeciesP. carotovorum, P. atrosepticum, P. parmentieri, P. brasiliense
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
Crystal Violet Pectate (CVP) Pectinolytic colonies produce characteristic pits (craters) in the medium within 48 h at 28°C[reference:39][reference:40]
LB Medium Colonies form within 24 h at 28°C; light brown to milky white, ~16–18 mm diameter after 4 days[reference:41][reference:42]
Nutrient Agar (NA) Shiny, grayish-white, round colonies; non-fluorescent[reference:43]
Logan's Medium Light red, 1.5-mm-diameter colonies; do not produce blue pigmented indigoidine[reference:44]
Cell Morphology Gram-negative, rod-shaped, motile (peritrichous flagella), facultative anaerobic[reference:45]
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample collection: Collect decayed stem, tuber, or bulb tissue showing soft rot symptoms.
  2. Surface sterilisation: Disinfect tissue with 2% NaClO (sodium hypochlorite), then rinse with sterile water[reference:46].
  3. Maceration: Macerate tissue in sterile water or saline solution.
  4. Plating: Streak onto Crystal Violet Pectate (CVP) medium for selective isolation of pectinolytic species. Alternatively, use LB or NA medium[reference:47].
  5. Incubation: Incubate at 25–28°C for 24–48 hours.
  6. Purification: Select colonies producing pits on CVP medium and restreak onto nutrient agar for purity[reference:48].
🔍 Identification Methods
  • Conventional PCR: Use genus-specific primers (e.g., Y1/Y2) targeting the 16S rRNA gene; expected amplicon ~430 bp. Species-specific primers for P. atrosepticum (ECA1f/ECA2r, ~690 bp) and P. carotovorum (EXPCCF/EXPCCR, ~550 bp).
  • Sequencing: Sequence the gapA, recA, or mdh genes for species-level identification.
  • Biochemical tests: Pectate degradation positive, phosphatase positive, growth at 37°C (varies by species), indigoidine production negative.
  • Pathogenicity test: Inoculate potato tuber slices or stems and observe maceration within 48 h[reference:49].
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Soft Rot (Potato, Vegetables)Water-soaked, mushy decay of tuber or stem tissue; tissue maceration with a foul odor.
Blackleg (Potato)Black discoloration and necrosis of stem base; wilting and yellowing of foliage.
Echeveria / OrnamentalsSoft rot of stems and leaves; collapse of succulent tissue.
Broccoli / AlliumSoft rot of heads and bulbs with grayish-white bacterial growth[reference:50].
Irish potato Echeveria Banana Vegetables

Curtobacterium flaccumfaciens (Bacterial Wilt of Beans)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumActinomycetota (Actinobacteria)
ClassActinomycetes
OrderMicrococcales
FamilyMicrobacteriaceae
GenusCurtobacterium
Speciesflaccumfaciens
Pathovarspv. flaccumfaciens, pv. poinsettiae, pv. oortii, pv. betae
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
Modified Burkholder's Agar (MBA) Convex, glistening, smooth-edged colonies with blue pigment diffusing into the medium (purple variant); yellow colonies producing extracellular blue pigment at 20 ± 2°C[reference:51]
Pseudomonas Agar F (PAF) + Sucrose Yellowish, circular with entire margins, flat to slightly convex, opaque, slightly fluidal[reference:52]
Nutrient Agar (NA) Yellow colonies after 7 days; circular, flat, entire, dull with filliform growth[reference:53][reference:54]
Cell Morphology Gram-positive, rod-shaped (~3 µm length), motile, aerobic[reference:55]
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample collection: Collect diseased bean seeds, stems, or leaves showing wilt or tan spot symptoms.
  2. Surface sterilisation: Surface-disinfect plant tissue or seeds with 70% ethanol followed by sterile water rinses.
  3. Maceration: Macerate tissue in sterile saline solution.
  4. Plating: Streak onto modified Burkholder's agar (MBA), Pseudomonas Agar F (PAF) with sucrose, or nutrient agar[reference:56].
  5. Incubation: Incubate at 20–25°C for 7–10 days (slow-growing).
  6. Purification: Select yellow colonies and restreak onto fresh medium for purity.
🔍 Identification Methods
  • Conventional PCR: Use Cff-specific primer set targeting the 16S rRNA gene or gyrB gene; expected amplicon ~300–500 bp[reference:57].
  • Biochemical tests: Gram-positive, aerobic, catalase positive, oxidase negative; yellow pigment production; blue pigment on MBA (purple variant)[reference:58].
  • Pathogenicity test: Inoculate susceptible bean varieties and observe wilt symptoms; reisolate to fulfil Koch's postulates.
  • Host range test: Differentiate pathovars based on host specificity (bean, poinsettia, tulip, beet).
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Bacterial Wilt (Bean)Wilting of leaves and stems, vascular discoloration, and stunting; plants may collapse.
Tan Spot (Soybean)Yellowish to brown lesions on leaves with dark margins; bacterial ooze under humid conditions.
Purple Seed StainPurple discoloration of seed coats; seeds may be shriveled.
Poinsettia (pv. poinsettiae)Stem cankers and leaf spots on poinsettia.
French beans (USA) Common bean Soybean Poinsettia

Pantoea stewartii (Stewart's Wilt of Corn)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
ClassGammaproteobacteria
OrderEnterobacterales
FamilyErwiniaceae
GenusPantoea
Speciesstewartii
Subspeciessubsp. stewartii, subsp. indologenes
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
Nutrient Glucose Agar (NGA) Cream-yellow to orange-yellow colonies; round, smooth, non-flat, mucoid[reference:59]
King's B Medium White or yellow pigment produced around colonies[reference:60]
Yeast Extract-Dextrose-CaCO₃ Agar (YDC) Yellow and convex colonies; colourless at first, becoming yellow[reference:61]
Nutrient Agar (NA) Small, round, slow-growing, yellow colonies; streaks vary from thin, yellow, moist to dry, orange-yellow[reference:62]
Cell Morphology Gram-negative, yellow-pigmented, mucoid, non-flagellate (subsp. stewartii is non-motile), non-spore-forming rod, 0.4–0.8 × 0.9–2.2 µm[reference:63]
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample collection: Collect leaves showing yellow stripes along veins, or stems with vascular discoloration[reference:64].
  2. Surface sterilisation: Wash and surface-disinfect leaf tissue.
  3. Maceration: Grind leaf tissue in sterile water or saline solution.
  4. Plating: Streak onto Nutrient Glucose Agar (NGA), King's B medium, or Yeast Extract-Dextrose-CaCO₃ agar.
  5. Incubation: Incubate at 28°C for 24–48 hours[reference:65].
  6. Purification: Select single yellow, mucoid colonies and restreak for purity.
🔍 Identification Methods
  • Conventional PCR: Use hrp gene or 16S–23S ITS primers; expected amplicon ~500–700 bp. Subspecies-specific primers differentiate subsp. stewartii from subsp. indologenes.
  • Real-time PCR (qPCR): TaqMan assays targeting the cps gene for sensitive detection in seed and leaf tissue.
  • Biochemical tests: Indole negative (subsp. stewartii), esculin hydrolysis negative, no growth on citrate, mucate, or DL-4-aminobutyrate[reference:66].
  • Motility test: subsp. stewartii is non-motile (lacks peritrichous flagella), distinguishing it from most other Pantoea species[reference:67].
  • Pathogenicity test: Inoculate susceptible corn seedlings and observe water-soaked lesions developing into yellow stripes.
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Stewart's Wilt (Corn)Yellow to white stripes along leaf veins; leaves may wilt and die; vascular discoloration of stems.
Seedling BlightInfected seeds may rot or produce stunted seedlings that wilt and die.
Bacterial OozeYellowish bacterial ooze visible on cut stems or lesion surfaces under humid conditions.
Jackfruit BronzingBronzing and discoloration of jackfruit perianth caused by subsp. indologenes.
Sweet corn (USA) Baby corn (USA) Maize Jackfruit

Rhodococcus fascians (Leafy Gall / Fasciation)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumActinomycetota (Actinobacteria)
ClassActinomycetes
OrderMycobacteriales (Corynebacteriales)
FamilyNocardiaceae
GenusRhodococcus
Speciesfascians (Tilford, 1936) Goodfellow, 1984
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
m-CFS Medium Yellow to orange, circular, convex, entire smooth colonies (2–4 mm diameter) after 4–7 days at 26°C; some strains form irregular, flat, rough colonies[reference:68]
Nutrient Agar (NA) Pinpoint to filamentous, rough, raised, orange colonies[reference:69]
D2 / NBY / GNA Media Typical colonies form on D2 agar, nutrient-broth yeast extract agar (NBY), and 1% glucose nutrient agar (GNA)[reference:70]
Cell Morphology Gram-positive, rod-shaped to coccoid, aerobic, non-motile; beige-red pigmentation on some media[reference:71]
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample collection: Collect symptomatic plant tissue showing leafy galls or fasciation (e.g., Zantedeschia, Iberis).
  2. Surface sterilisation: Wash tissue and surface-disinfect with 70% ethanol followed by sterile water rinses.
  3. Maceration: Macerate tissue in sterile saline solution.
  4. Plating: Streak onto m-CFS medium (selective for Rhodococcus), D2 agar, NBY, or GNA[reference:72].
  5. Incubation: Incubate at 26°C for 4–7 days (slow-growing).
  6. Purification: Select single yellow-to-orange colonies and restreak for purity.
🔍 Identification Methods
  • Conventional PCR: Use fasA or fasB gene primers (fasciation-associated genes) for specific detection; expected amplicon ~400–600 bp.
  • Sequencing: Sequence the 16S rRNA gene for genus/species confirmation.
  • Biochemical tests: Gram-positive, catalase positive, oxidase negative, aerobic; yellow-orange pigmentation.
  • Pathogenicity test: Inoculate susceptible host plants (e.g., Zantedeschia, Iberis) and observe leafy gall or fasciation symptoms.
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Leafy Gall (Zantedeschia)Abnormal proliferation of shoots and leaves at the base of the plant, forming a leafy gall or "witches' broom".
FasciationFlattened, thickened, and elongated stems with excessive branching; flowers may be deformed.
Stunted GrowthReduced plant vigour and distortion of growing tips.
Iberis sempervirensFasciation and leafy gall formation on stems and crowns[reference:73].
Zantedeschia Iberis sempervirens Various ornamentals

Pseudomonas syringae (Bacterial Blight / Leaf Spot)

▼
🔬 Classification
DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
ClassGammaproteobacteria
OrderPseudomonadales
FamilyPseudomonadaceae
GenusPseudomonas
Speciessyringae
Pathovarspv. syringae, pv. phaseolicola, pv. theae, pv. persicae, and many others
🧫 Morphological Properties on Plates
King's B Medium Creamy white, flat, circular colonies (4.5–5 mm diameter) producing a pale blue fluorescent pigment under UV light[reference:74]
Selective Medium (Kiwifruit) Convex colonies with entire margins; purple centre and opalescent white margin[reference:75]
Tea Blight Medium Opalescent colonies; "fried-egg" type colony with granular and fibril structures when viewed under oblique light after 2 days[reference:76]
Milk-Tween Agar Creamy white, flat, circular colonies with pale blue fluorescent pigment under UV[reference:77]
Cell Morphology Gram-negative, rod-shaped, motile (one to several polar flagella), aerobic
🧪 Isolation Protocol
  1. Sample collection: Collect diseased leaves with water-soaked lesions or blighted tissue.
  2. Surface sterilisation: Wash leaves and surface-disinfect with 70% ethanol.
  3. Maceration: Cut small segments from lesion margins and macerate in sterile water.
  4. Plating: Streak onto King's B medium, Milk-Tween agar, or semi-selective media (e.g., DTarTA for pv. maculicola)[reference:78].
  5. Incubation: Incubate at 24–25°C for 2–4 days[reference:79].
  6. Purification: Select single colonies and restreak for purity.
🔍 Identification Methods
  • Conventional PCR: Use pathovar-specific primers targeting hrp genes, avr genes, or the 16S–23S ITS region. Expected amplicons vary by pathovar (300–700 bp).
  • Real-time PCR (qPCR): TaqMan assays for sensitive detection and quantification.
  • Biochemical tests: Oxidase negative, catalase positive, fluorescent pigment production on King's B, levan production positive.
  • Hypersensitive Response (HR) Test: Infiltrate tobacco leaves with bacterial suspension; positive HR (necrosis within 24 h) confirms pathogenicity.
  • Pathogenicity test: Inoculate susceptible host plants and observe lesion development.
🌿 Signs on Affected Crops
Bacterial Blight (Kiwifruit)Water-soaked lesions on leaves and blossoms; cankers on canes with reddish exudate.
Halo Blight (Bean)Small water-soaked spots that develop into necrotic lesions with yellow halos; caused by pv. phaseolicola.
Bacterial Shoot Blight (Tea)Water-soaked lesions on young shoots that turn brown and necrotic.
Bacterial Canker (Stone Fruits)Cankers on stems and branches with gumming; caused by pv. persicae and pv. syringae.
Gossypium (Cotton) Kiwifruit Bean Tea Stone fruits

General Best Practices: Always include positive and negative controls in PCR and ELISA assays. For soil and water samples, use semi-selective media to reduce saprophytic competition. Confirm presumptive isolates with at least two independent methods (e.g., PCR + pathogenicity test) before reporting a definitive identification. Maintain pure cultures on appropriate media slants or in glycerol stocks at −80°C for long-term preservation.

Bacterial Pathogen Diagnostic Notes — Compiled from standard microbiological and molecular diagnostic protocols.

Always refer to validated diagnostic protocols (EPPO, ISTA, IPPC) and regional guidelines for regulatory testing and confirmation.
