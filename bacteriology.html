Bacteriology

Bacteriology is the branch of microbiology that studies the morphology, ecology, genetics, and biochemistry of bacteria. It also examines the ways bacteria cause disease and how they can be used in industry, medicine, and agriculture.

Key Areas of Study

  • Bacterial cell structure and function
  • Growth, reproduction, and metabolism
  • Classification and identification
  • Pathogenesis and the immune response
  • Antibiotics and antimicrobial resistance

Milestones

  • 1676 — Antonie van Leeuwenhoek observes bacteria under a microscope
  • 1876 — Robert Koch links a specific bacterium to anthrax
  • 1928 — Alexander Fleming discovers penicillin

“Bacteria are the most abundant form of life on Earth.”
