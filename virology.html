1 Potato Viruses

Viruses detected in Irish potato and potato samples from Nairobi, Nyandarua prisons, ATC Oljororok, Kabete, North Narok, TC Lab, and the Netherlands.

Potato leafroll virus PLRV

Polerovirus Solemoviridae
Classification

Genus Polerovirus, family Solemoviridae (formerly Luteoviridae). Isometric particles ~24 nm. Phloem-restricted, positive-sense ssRNA genome.

Spread
  • Transmitted by more than 10 aphid species in a persistent, circulative, non-propagative manner. Myzus persicae is the principal vector.
  • Not sap-transmissible.
  • Spreads long-distance through infected seed tubers.
  • Primary infection (current season) causes upward rolling of young leaves; secondary infection (from infected tubers) causes pronounced leaf rolling and erect plant growth.
Identification
  • ELISA (DAS-ELISA) is the standard serological method for leaves, sprouts, and tubers. Tuber ELISA can underestimate incidence compared with grow-out tests.
  • RT-PCR for confirmatory detection, especially in dormant tubers.
  • Symptoms: leaf pallor, upward rolling, upright habit, leathery leaves.

Potato virus A PVA

Potyvirus Potyviridae
Classification

Genus Potyvirus, family Potyviridae. Flexuous filamentous particles. Positive-sense ssRNA.

Spread
  • Transmitted by at least 7 aphid species in a non-persistent, stylet-borne manner. Key vectors: Myzus persicae, Macrosiphum euphorbiae, Aphis frangulae, A. nasturtii.
  • Acquisition and transmission occur in less than one minute, with no latent period.
  • Also transmissible by sap inoculation.
  • Host range restricted to Solanaceae.
Identification
  • ELISA with PVA-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR for strain-level discrimination.
  • Difficult to distinguish from PVY by electron microscopy alone; serology or molecular methods required.
  • Symptoms: mild mosaic, yield reduction up to 40% in susceptible cultivars.

Potato virus X PVX

Potexvirus Alphaflexiviridae
Classification

Genus Potexvirus, family Alphaflexiviridae. Flexuous filamentous particles. Type member of the genus.

Spread
  • Mechanical transmission is the primary route — through contact between plants, contaminated tools, machinery, and farming operations.
  • Not transmitted by aphids.
  • Can be transmitted through pollen, true seed, and contaminated farming equipment.
  • Some transmission by zoospores of Synchytrium endobioticum and grasshopper bites has been reported.
Identification
  • ELISA (DAS-ELISA) — reliable due to high virus concentration in host plants.
  • RT-PCR and dot blot with DIG-labelled DNA probes.
  • Slide precipitin test: flocculent (flagellar) precipitates.
  • Symptoms: many strains are symptomless or cause only mild mosaic. Severe strains may cause necrosis.

Potato virus Y PVY

Potyvirus Potyviridae
Classification

Genus Potyvirus, family Potyviridae. Type species of the genus. Flexuous filamentous particles.

Spread
  • Transmitted by more than 40 aphid species in a non-persistent manner. Myzus persicae is the most efficient vector. Cereal aphids may also contribute to epidemics.
  • Also spread through planting of contaminated seed tubers.
  • Rapid acquisition and transmission — aphids can acquire and transmit within seconds.
Strain complexity
  • Major strains: PVYO, PVYC, PVYN, PVYE, PVYZ, PVYD.
  • Recombinant strains: PVYNTN, PVYN-Wi (syn. PVYN:O).
  • PVYNTN causes potato tuber necrotic ringspot disease (PTNRD) — necrotic tuber spots.
  • PVYN and PVYNTN produce necrotic leaf spots; PVYO causes mosaic discolorations.
Identification
  • ELISA — most widely used, relatively low cost.
  • RT-PCR — essential for strain discrimination. Multiplex RT-PCR can differentiate PVYO, PVYN, PVYC, and PVYNTN in a single assay.
  • RT-qPCR — best for detecting mixed infections and quantifying strain concentrations.
  • AmpliDet RNA — simultaneous detection and typing.

Potato virus M PVM

Carlavirus Betaflexiviridae
Classification

Genus Carlavirus, family Betaflexiviridae. Flexuous filamentous particles ~650 × 12–13 nm. Positive-sense ssRNA.

Spread
  • Transmitted by aphids in a non-persistent manner. Principal vector: Myzus persicae; less efficiently by Aphis frangulae, A. nasturtii, Macrosiphum euphorbiae.
  • Also sap-transmissible to a limited range of species.
  • Transmitted through tubers.
Identification
  • ELISA with PVM-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR for confirmation.
  • Symptoms: mild to severe mosaic, leaf distortion; often symptomless in many cultivars.

Potato virus S PVS

Carlavirus Betaflexiviridae
Classification

Genus Carlavirus, family Betaflexiviridae. Flexuous filamentous particles. Positive-sense ssRNA.

Strain groups
  • PVSO (Ordinary) — mild, largely symptomless.
  • PVSA (Andean) — severe symptoms including premature senescence and defoliation; more efficiently aphid-transmitted than PVSO. Classified as a quarantine parasite in Europe.
Spread
  • Contact transmission — very easily spread between diseased and healthy plants via farm implements and plant-to-plant contact.
  • Aphid transmission — non-persistent manner (some isolates).
  • Transmitted through tubers.
Identification
  • ELISA — routine detection.
  • RT-PCR — essential for distinguishing PVSO from PVSA.
  • Symptoms: often latent; deepening of leaf veins, roughness of leaf surface, mosaic patterns in severe cases.

Andean potato latent virus APLV

Tymovirus Tymoviridae EPPO A1
Classification

Genus Tymovirus, family Tymoviridae. Isometric particles. Positive-sense ssRNA. EPPO A1 quarantine pest.

Spread
  • Beetle-transmitted — typical of tymoviruses.
  • Also mechanically transmissible by sap inoculation.
  • Not transmitted by aphids.
Identification
  • ELISA with APLV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR and sequencing of coat protein gene.
Note: APLV and Andean potato mild mosaic virus (APMMV) are now recognised as distinct species; species-level discrimination requires molecular methods.

Potato black ringspot virus PBRSV

Nepovirus Secoviridae EPPO A1
Classification

Genus Nepovirus, family Secoviridae. Subgroup A based on RNA2 size and genome organisation. EPPO A1 quarantine pest.

Spread
  • Nematode-transmitted — by Xiphinema species (dagger nematodes).
  • Also transmitted through vegetative propagules.
  • Seed and pollen transmission documented in some hosts.
Identification
  • ELISA with PBRSV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR and sequencing of RNA1 and RNA2 for confirmation.
  • Symptoms: black ringspots on tubers, calico symptoms.

Potato virus T PVT

Tepovirus Betaflexiviridae
Classification

Genus Tepovirus, family Betaflexiviridae. Type species of the genus. Flexuous filamentous particles. Positive-sense ssRNA.

Spread
  • Mechanically transmissible by sap inoculation.
  • No known vector.
  • Transmitted through tubers.
Identification
  • ELISA and RT-PCR.
  • Genome ~6,539 nt with three ORFs.
  • Limited literature on detection; molecular confirmation recommended.

Potato virus Y strain NTN PVYNTN

Potyvirus Potyviridae Recombinant strain
Classification

Recombinant strain of PVY, genus Potyvirus, family Potyviridae.

Spread
  • Same as PVY — non-persistent aphid transmission.
  • Seed tuber transmission.
Identification
  • ELISA — can detect PVY but cannot discriminate NTN from other strains.
  • Multiplex RT-PCR — specific detection of PVYNTN and discrimination from PVYO, PVYN, PVYC.
  • RT-qPCR — best for quantification and mixed infections.
  • Symptoms: necrotic leaf spots and necrotic tuber spots (PTNRD).

2 Sweet Potato Viruses

Viruses detected in sweet potato samples from greenhouse sources. SPCSV, SPMMV and SPFMV were all recorded in the same sample set — the combination of SPCSV with SPFMV constitutes the severe sweet potato virus disease (SPVD) complex.

Sweet potato chlorotic stunt virus SPCSV

Crinivirus Closteroviridae
Classification

Genus Crinivirus, family Closteroviridae. Bipartite genome (RNA1 ~9,407 nt; RNA2 ~8,223 nt) — second largest genome among plant viruses after Citrus tristeza virus. Phloem-limited.

Spread
  • Whitefly-transmitted (Bemisia tabaci, Trialeurodes spp.).
  • Also spread through infected sweet potato vines and storage roots.
  • Occurs in all major tropical regions; most damaging pathogen of sweet potato in Africa.
Identification
  • ELISA with SPCSV-specific antiserum (serotypes EA1, EA2, WA).
  • RT-PCR targeting the HSP70 gene or coat protein region.
Note: SPCSV is a component of the severe sweet potato virus disease (SPVD) complex when co-infecting with SPFMV.

Sweet potato mild mottle virus SPMMV

Ipomovirus Potyviridae
Classification

Genus Ipomovirus, family Potyviridae. Distinct genus within the family. Flexuous filamentous particles. Positive-sense ssRNA.

Spread
  • Whitefly-transmitted (Bemisia tabaci) — unique among potyvirids, most of which are aphid-transmitted.
  • Also mechanically transmissible.
  • Spread through infected planting material.
Identification
  • ELISA with SPMMV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR targeting the coat protein or NIb region.
  • Symptoms: leaf mottling, stunting, yield loss.

Sweet potato feathery mottle virus SPFMV

Potyvirus Potyviridae
Classification

Genus Potyvirus, family Potyviridae. Most common sweet potato virus worldwide. Positive-sense ssRNA.

Strain groups
  • EA (East Africa), RC (Russet Crack), O (Ordinary), C (now reclassified as Sweet potato virus C — SPVC).
Spread
  • Aphid-transmitted in a non-persistent manner.
  • Also spread through infected vines and storage roots.
  • SPFMV is a component of the SPVD complex with SPCSV.
Identification
  • ELISA — reliable only in leaves with symptoms; virus is unevenly distributed. Sample multiple leaves per plant.
  • RT-PCR — essential for strain discrimination and for distinguishing SPFMV from SPVC.
  • Symptoms: feathery mottling, vein clearing, leaf distortion.

3 Other Viruses

Viruses detected in strawberry, raspberry, blackberry, blueberry, maize, Hydrangea, Matthiola, Callistephus and Helichrysium samples, plus the grow-out indexing method.

Cucumber mosaic virus CMV

Cucumovirus Bromoviridae
Classification

Genus Cucumovirus, family Bromoviridae. Type species of the genus. Isometric particles. Tripartite genome (RNAs 1, 2, 3). Subgroups I and II based on serology and sequence.

Spread
  • Aphid-transmitted in a non-persistent manner — many aphid species.
  • Also seed-transmitted in some hosts.
  • Sap-transmissible by mechanical inoculation.
  • Widest host range of any plant virus: >1,200 species in >100 plant families.
Identification
  • ELISA with CMV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR — subgroup discrimination (I vs II) via coat protein gene sequencing.
  • Rapid field detection kits — available for few-minute detection in the field.
  • Symptoms: mosaic, mottling, stunting, fernleaf in some hosts.

Tomato spotted wilt virus TSWV

Orthotospovirus Tospoviridae
Classification

Genus Orthotospovirus (formerly Tospovirus), family Tospoviridae (formerly Bunyaviridae). Type species. Enveloped, spherical particles. Tripartite genome (L, M, S RNAs). Negative-sense or ambisense ssRNA.

Spread
  • Thrips-transmitted in a circulative and propagative manner. Key vectors: Frankliniella occidentalis (western flower thrips), Thrips tabaci.
  • Thrips must acquire the virus as larvae to transmit as adults.
  • Also spread through infected planting material.
Identification
  • ELISA with TSWV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR targeting the N gene (S RNA).
  • LAMP — rapid field-deployable detection.
  • Symptoms: necrotic ringspots, yellowing, browning, chlorotic rings on leaves and fruits.

Tobacco streak virus TSV

Ilarvirus Bromoviridae
Classification

Genus Ilarvirus, family Bromoviridae. Type species of the genus. Subgroup I. Quasi-spherical virions. Tripartite genome (RNA1, RNA2, RNA3). Positive-sense ssRNA.

Spread
  • Seed- and pollen-transmitted in certain host species.
  • Thrips-assisted transmission — thrips feeding on pollen-contaminated plants can transmit the virus.
  • Also mechanically transmissible by sap inoculation.
Identification
  • ELISA with TSV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR targeting the coat protein gene.
  • Symptoms: necrotic streaks, ringspots, stunting; often symptomless in some hosts.

Tobacco ringspot virus TRSV

Nepovirus Secoviridae
Classification

Genus Nepovirus, family Secoviridae. Type species of the genus. Subgroup A. Isometric particles. Bipartite genome (RNA1, RNA2).

Spread
  • Nematode-transmitted — by Xiphinema americanum and closely related Xiphinema species. Nematodes acquire the virus within 24 hours; larvae and adults transmit efficiently.
  • Also transmitted by thrips, spider mites, grasshoppers, flea beetles, and possibly aphids.
  • Seed and pollen transmission documented.
  • Spreads worldwide through vegetative propagules of perennial crops and ornamentals.
Identification
  • ELISA with TRSV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR targeting the coat protein or movement protein genes.
  • Symptoms: ringspots, mottling, necrosis, stunting; wide host range including herbaceous and woody plants.

Maize chlorotic mottle virus MCMV

Machlomovirus Tombusviridae
Classification

Genus Machlomovirus, family Tombusviridae. Type species. Small isometric particles. Positive-sense ssRNA.

Spread
  • Chrysomelid beetle-transmitted in a semi-persistent manner. Beetles retain transmissibility for up to 6 days.
  • Also thrips-transmitted (Frankliniella williamsii, F. occidentalis).
  • Mechanically transmissible by sap inoculation.
  • Seed and soil transmission documented.
Note: MCMV is a component of corn lethal necrosis when co-infecting with Sugarcane mosaic virus (SCMV) or other potyviruses.
Identification
  • ELISA with MCMV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR targeting the coat protein gene.
  • Symptoms: chlorotic mottling, necrosis, stunting, death.

Sugarcane mosaic virus SCMV

Potyvirus Potyviridae
Classification

Genus Potyvirus, family Potyviridae. Sugarcane mosaic subgroup. Flexuous filamentous particles ~750 nm. Positive-sense ssRNA.

Spread
  • Aphid-transmitted in a non-persistent manner — several aphid species.
  • Mechanically transmissible by sap inoculation.
  • Spread through infected sugarcane stalk cuttings (vegetative propagation).
  • Infects maize, sorghum, sugarcane, and other poaceous species.
Identification
  • ELISA with SCMV-specific antiserum.
  • RT-PCR targeting the coat protein or NIb region.
  • Symptoms: mosaic, mottling, chlorosis, stunting.

“Grow out test” Method

Not a virus — a testing method
Definition

A biological indexing method where dormant potato tubers are planted and grown for 6–8 weeks to allow virus symptoms to develop. Virus presence is then assessed visually and/or by ELISA on the resulting leaves.

Use in these samples
  • Submitted for Matthiola, Callistephus, and Helichrysium samples from the Netherlands, Chile, and China.
  • Provides a comprehensive check for multiple viruses, including those not detected by routine ELISA on dormant tubers.
  • Compared with direct tuber testing (ELISA, RT-PCR), grow-out tests are slower but can detect unevenly distributed viruses and mixed infections.
Limitations
  • Time-consuming (6–8 weeks).
  • Requires greenhouse space.
  • Symptom expression may be masked by environmental conditions or cultivar resistance.

5 Key Identification Methods — Laboratory Workflow

Methods available for the detection, confirmation and typing of the viruses listed above.

Detection and identification methods by target and use case
Method Target Use case
ELISA (DAS / TAS) Viral coat protein Routine screening, high-throughput
RT-PCR Viral RNA Confirmatory detection, strain discrimination
RT-qPCR Viral RNA Quantification, mixed infections
Multiplex RT-PCR Multiple targets Simultaneous strain typing (e.g. PVYO/N/C/NTN)
LAMP Viral RNA Rapid field-deployable detection
Grow-out test Biological indexing Comprehensive virus screening in dormant tubers
Sequencing Full genome Novel virus characterisation, taxonomy
Workflow principle: routine screening by ELISA → confirmation and typing by RT-PCR / multiplex RT-PCR → quantification and mixed-infection assessment by RT-qPCR → sequencing where species or strain identity remains unresolved, or where a quarantine pest is suspected.
