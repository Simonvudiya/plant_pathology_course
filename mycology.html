1 Fungal Wilt & Root Rot Pathogens

Pathogens detected in soil, water, beans, pawpaw, coconut, avocado and Irish potato samples — including vascular wilt agents, anthracnose, charcoal rot and Phytophthora species.

Fusarium wilt (Fusarium spp.)

Ascomycota Nectriaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Ascomycota, Class Sordariomycetes, Order Hypocreales, Family Nectriaceae. Teleomorph: Gibberella for some species. Key species: Fusarium oxysporum (vascular wilt), F. solani (root rot / dry rot), F. equiseti.

Description

Fusarium spp. produce septate hyphae and characteristic canoe-shaped macroconidia. F. oxysporum produces abundant microconidia on short phialides and chlamydospores. Colonies on PDA range from pale violet to alizarine pink to old rose, depending on the species and isolate. F. solani produces stout macroconidia with blunt, rounded ends.

Isolation

Isolate from vascular tissue of wilted plants or from diseased tubers. Surface-sterilise tissue in 1% NaOCl for 1–2 min, rinse in sterile water, and plate on potato dextrose agar (PDA) or Komada’s selective medium for Fusarium spp. For F. oxysporum from soil, use dilution plating on PCNB medium. Incubate at 25°C for 5–7 days.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on macroconidia shape, microconidia presence/absence, chlamydospore formation, and colony pigmentation.
  • F. oxysporum is distinguished by the absence of chlamydospores in some isolates and the presence of microconidia in false heads.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing or TEF-1α (translation elongation factor 1-alpha) gene sequencing.
  • F. oxysporum formae speciales are distinguished by pathogenicity testing on differential hosts.

Anthracnose (Colletotrichum lindemuthianum)

Ascomycota Glomerellaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Ascomycota, Class Sordariomycetes, Order Glomerellales, Family Glomerellaceae. Synonyms: Colletotrichum lindemuthianum (Sacc. & Magnus) Briosi & Cavara. Common names: bean anthracnose, black spot, fruit rot.

Description

Produces septate mycelium — hyaline when young, turning dark brown to black with age. Conidia are hyaline, single-celled, with one vacuole, measuring approximately 5.7 × 2.0 µm. Acervuli form on infected tissue, producing setae and conidia in pinkish masses under humid conditions.

Isolation

Collect bean stems, leaves, or pods showing typical anthracnose lesions (dark, sunken, circular spots with reddish-brown margins). Surface-sterilise in 1% NaOCl, rinse, and plate on PDA or bean extract agar. Incubate at 22–25°C for 5–7 days. Isolates are purified by single-spore or hyphal tip transfer.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on acervuli, setae, and conidial dimensions.
  • C. lindemuthianum is distinguished from other Colletotrichum spp. by host range and conidial morphology.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and β-tubulin (TUB2) gene sequencing.
  • Race identification is performed on a set of 12 standard differential bean cultivars. Molecular markers (SCAR, RAPD) are also available for race characterisation.

Charcoal rot (Macrophomina phaseolina)

Ascomycota Botryosphaeriaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Ascomycota, Class Dothideomycetes, Order Botryosphaeriales, Family Botryosphaeriaceae. Anamorphic fungus; teleomorph not commonly observed.

Description

Fast-growing colonies on PDA, initially white, turning dark brown to black as sclerotia form. Produces abundant dark brown to black mycelium and microsclerotia (black, hard, spherical structures). Optimal growth temperature 35°C; growth range 15–40°C. Causes charcoal rot, ashy stem rot, and seedling blight.

Isolation

Isolate from diseased stem tissue or roots. Surface-sterilise tissue, rinse, and plate on PDA or potato carrot agar. Incubate at 30–35°C for 3–5 days. From soil, use dilution plating or sclerotia flotation in sucrose solution. The fungus is a soil-borne necrotrophic pathogen with a wide host range (>500 species).

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on fast growth, dark mycelium, and microsclerotia formation.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing; the ITS region is widely used for species-level identification.
  • Pathogenicity testing on susceptible hosts (e.g. soybean, maize) confirms identity.

Ganoderma boninense

Basidiomycota Ganodermataceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Basidiomycota, Class Agaricomycetes, Order Polyporales, Family Ganodermataceae.

Description

Ganoderma boninense is the primary causal agent of basal stem rot (BSR) in oil palm. Produces large, shelf-like basidiocarps (fruiting bodies) with a characteristic shiny, laccate (varnished) surface. The context is woody and brown. Causes white rot of the lower stem and root tissues.

Isolation

Isolate from diseased stem tissue at the advancing margin of decay. Surface-sterilise, cut into small pieces, and plate on PDA or Ganoderma selective medium (with benomyl or PCNB). Incubate at 25–30°C for 7–14 days. From soil, use baiting with oil palm wood blocks or rubber wood segments.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on basidiocarp morphology and basidiospore characteristics.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and TEF-1α sequencing.
  • G. boninense is distinguished from other Ganoderma species by host specificity and molecular markers.

Marasmius palmivorus

Basidiomycota Marasmiaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Basidiomycota, Class Agaricomycetes, Order Agaricales, Family Marasmiaceae. Formerly classified as Marasmius palmivorus (Sharples, 1928); now recognised as Paramarasmius palmivorus (Sharples) Antonín & Kolářík, 2022.

Description

A marasmioid fungus that exhibits parasitic behaviour, although most marasmioids are rarely parasitic. Causes fruit bunch rot of oil palm, white root rot, and collar rot of various hosts. Produces small, pale basidiocarps with a marasmioid habit. Capable of breaking down cellulose and hemicellulose in plant tissue, while only slightly modifying lignin.

Isolation

Isolate from basidiocarps or diseased fruitlets of oil palm. Surface-sterilise tissue, plate on PDA or MEA, and incubate at 25°C for 7–14 days. From soil, use dilution plating or baiting with sterile wood pieces.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on basidiocarp morphology and hyphal characteristics.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and mtSSU sequencing; ITS and mtSSU sequences have confirmed identity to Paramarasmius palmivorus with 99.9–99.8% identity.
  • Phylogenetic analysis of ITS rDNA is the standard molecular method.

Phytophthora palmivora

Oomycota Peronosporaceae Not a true fungus
Classification

Kingdom Chromista, Phylum Oomycota, Class Peronosporomycetes (Oomycetes), Order Peronosporales, Family Peronosporaceae. Note: Phytophthora is an oomycete, not a true fungus.

Description

Produces elliptical to ovoid, prominently papillate, caducous sporangia with a mean pedicel length of approximately 5 mm. Short pedicels average 3.13 µm ± 0.28, with a sporangia length/width ratio ≥1.4. Causes black pod rot of cacao, bud rot of coconut, and root rot of various hosts. Colonies on selective media are slow-growing compared to many fungi.

Isolation

Isolate from infected plant tissue (roots, pods, buds) or from soil/water sources. Use selective media such as PARP (pimaricin, ampicillin, rifampicin, PCNB) or CMA-PARPH. From soil, use baiting with apple or pear fruits, or dilution plating. Incubate at 20–25°C for 3–7 days.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on sporangia shape (elliptical to ovoid, papillate), caducity, pedicel length, and chlamydospore formation.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing, COX1, and COX2 barcoding.
  • P. palmivora is grouped in clade 1 of the genus Phytophthora.
  • Isozyme analysis (GPI patterns) can differentiate isolates from different hosts.

Phytophthora infestans

Oomycota Peronosporaceae Not a true fungus
Classification

Kingdom Chromista, Phylum Oomycota, Class Peronosporomycetes, Order Peronosporales, Family Peronosporaceae. Heterothallic oomycete with two mating types: A1 and A2.

Description

Causal agent of late blight of potato and tomato. Produces lemon-shaped sporangia, zoospores, and chlamydospores in some isolates. The life cycle may involve up to three asexual spore forms. Heterothallic with two known mating types; A1 and A2 isolates can mate to produce oospores.

Isolation

Isolate from leaves, stems, or tubers with late blight symptoms using the dampening chamber method, followed by cultivation on nutrient media. Use rye B agar or PDA amended with antibiotics. Incubate at 18–22°C for 7–14 days. For tuber tissue, surface-sterilise and plate directly.

Identification
  • Morphological identification by microscopic analysis of sporangia and sporangiophores.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and mitochondrial loci analysis.
  • Mating type determination by pairing with known A1 and A2 tester isolates.
  • Allozyme analysis at the Gpi locus and Pep locus is used for genotype characterisation.

2 Soilborne & Tuber-Borne Pathogens

Pathogens detected in soil, water and Irish potato samples — including Rhizoctonia, Phoma, Spongospora, Synchytrium, Verticillium and Sclerotinia.

Rhizoctonia solani

Basidiomycota Ceratobasidiaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Basidiomycota, Class Agaricomycetes, Order Cantharellales, Family Ceratobasidiaceae. Teleomorph: Thanatephorus cucumeris (anamorph = Rhizoctonia solani). Classified into 13 anastomosis groups (AGs) based on hyphal anastomosis reactions.

Description

Soilborne basidiomycetous pathogen. Forms buff to brown mycelial mats on PDA, whereas other Rhizoctonia species form white to buff to salmon-coloured mats. Hyphae are wide, septate, and branch at right angles — a key diagnostic feature distinguishing it from Pythium and Phytophthora. Produces sclerotia (brown to black, irregular).

Isolation

Isolate from diseased tissue or soil. Surface-sterilise tissue, rinse, and plate on PDA or water agar (for hyphal tip isolation). From soil, use dilution plating on Ko and Hora’s selective medium or Rhizoctonia selective medium (with chloramphenicol, streptomycin, and PCNB). Incubate at 25–28°C for 3–5 days.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on hyphal morphology (right-angle branching, septate hyphae, no clamp connections).
  • Anastomosis grouping is the standard method for subdividing strains.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and RAPD analysis.
  • Fatty acid profiling and pectin enzyme analysis are additional tools.

Phoma exigua var. foveata (Potato gangrene)

Ascomycota Didymellaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Ascomycota, Class Dothideomycetes, Order Pleosporales, Family Didymellaceae. Current name: Boeremia foveata (Foister) Aveskamp, Gruyter & Verkley. Synonyms: Phoma foveata Foister, Phoma solanicola f. foveata.

Description

Causes potato gangrene. P. exigua var. foveata and P. exigua var. exigua are morphologically indistinguishable; both are pathogenic to potato tubers when inoculated into wounded tissue. Produces dark, sunken lesions on tubers. Differentiated by colour reaction on malt extract agar: P. exigua var. foveata produces a green to red discolouration, while P. exigua var. exigua produces a green to red discolouration (different reaction).

Isolation

Isolate from gangrenous tuber tissue. Cut small pieces from the advancing margin of lesions, surface-sterilise, and plate on PDA or malt extract agar. Incubate at 20–22°C for 7–10 days. Pycnidia form after 10–14 days.

Identification
  • Morphological identification is difficult due to similarity with P. exigua var. exigua.
  • Immunofluorescence can differentiate between the two varieties.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and TUB2 sequencing.
  • Pathogenicity testing on wounded potato tubers is confirmatory.

Spongospora subterranea f.sp. subterranea

Cercozoa Plasmodiophoraceae Not culturable
Classification

Kingdom Protista (Protozoa), Phylum Cercozoa, Subphylum Endomyxa, Class Phytomyxea, Order Plasmodiophorida, Family Plasmodiophoraceae. Note: Spongospora is a plasmodiophorid, not a true fungus. Obligate biotroph — does not grow on any culture media.

Description

Causes powdery scab of potato tubers. Also transmits Potato mop-top virus (PMTV). Infection occurs through lenticels on tubers, resulting in raised outgrowths that eventually shrink and form powdery masses of resting spores (cystosori). Resting spores are aggregated into cystosori (spongy, honeycomb-like structures).

Isolation

Cannot be cultured on artificial media — obligate biotroph. Detection from soil or tuber tissue by baiting with susceptible potato plants, or by direct microscopic examination of tuber lesions for cystosori. Single cystosorus isolation is possible for molecular characterisation.

Identification
  • Morphological identification by microscopic examination of cystosori and resting spores.
  • Molecular confirmation by PCR and RFLP analysis of the ITS region.
  • Three ribotypes (I, II, III) have been identified, with ribotype I found in South America.
  • Soil detection by PCR-based assays targeting the ITS region.

Synchytrium endobioticum (Potato wart disease)

Chytridiomycota Synchytriaceae EPPO A1
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Chytridiomycota, Class Chytridiomycetes, Order Chytridiales, Family Synchytriaceae. Obligate biotroph. EPPO A1 quarantine pest.

Description

Causes potato wart disease. Produces galls (warts) on potato tubers, stolons, and stems. Resting spores (sporangia) are thick-walled and survive in soil for decades. Not culturable on artificial media — obligate biotroph.

Isolation

Cannot be cultured on artificial media. Detection from soil or tuber tissue by direct microscopic examination for resting spores, or by baiting with susceptible potato cultivars in soil samples. Grow-out tests in quarantine facilities are used for diagnosis.

Identification
  • Morphological identification by microscopic examination of resting spores (sporangia) from warts or soil.
  • Molecular confirmation by PCR and sequencing of the ITS region.
  • Because of its quarantine status, suspected cases must be confirmed by a reference laboratory using molecular and biological methods.
Quarantine note: Synchytrium endobioticum is an EPPO A1 quarantine pest. Any suspected detection must be reported immediately and confirmed by a designated reference laboratory.

Verticillium dahliae

Ascomycota Plectosphaerellaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Ascomycota, Class Sordariomycetes, Order Glomerellales, Family Plectosphaerellaceae. Two pathotypes described: non-defoliating and defoliating.

Description

Produces white colonies with abundant fluffy mycelium on PDA. Maximum growth at 25°C. Produces microsclerotia (black, irregular, resting structures) — a key distinguishing feature from V. albo-atrum (which produces dark resting mycelium). Conidia are short (<6.0 µm), distinguishing it from V. longisporum (conidia >6.1 µm). Causes vascular wilt in many crops including cotton, potato, olive, and ornamentals.

Isolation

Isolate from vascular tissue of wilted plants. Surface-sterilise, cut into small pieces, and plate on PDA or ethanol-streptomycin agar (for selective isolation). Incubate at 22–25°C for 7–14 days. From soil, use dilution plating on pectate medium or sorbose medium.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on microsclerotia formation, conidial dimensions, and colony morphology.
  • Pathotype determination by inoculation on differential hosts (cotton, okra, olive).
  • Molecular confirmation by PCR using primer pairs DB19/DB22, which produce DNA bands of 539 or 523 bp.
  • VCG (vegetative compatibility group) analysis and multilocus phylogenetic analysis are used for strain characterisation.

Sclerotinia spp.

Ascomycota Sclerotiniaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Ascomycota, Class Leotiomycetes, Order Helotiales, Family Sclerotiniaceae. Key species: Sclerotinia sclerotiorum, S. minor, S. trifoliorum. Teleomorph: Sclerotinia; anamorph: not commonly used.

Description

Causes white mould disease with a wide distribution worldwide. Produces white, cottony mycelium and large, black, irregular sclerotia on the soil surface and within infected tissues. S. sclerotiorum forms larger sclerotia than S. minor. Colonies on PDA produce darkly pigmented mycelium in some isolates. Host range includes crop and weed species.

Isolation

Isolate from diseased stem tissue or sclerotia. Surface-sterilise, rinse, and plate on PDA or water agar. Incubate at 20–22°C for 5–7 days. Sclerotia germinate to produce mycelium or apothecia (depending on conditions). From soil, use sclerotia flotation or sieving.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on sclerotial size and shape, ascospore and ascus dimensions, and host range.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing; the ITS region reliably distinguishes S. sclerotiorum, S. minor, and S. trifoliorum.
  • Single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP)-based diagnostic systems are available for species-level discrimination.

3 Downy Mildews, Smuts & Rusts

Pathogens detected in sweet corn, baby corn and Sanguisorba samples — including obligate biotrophs that cannot be cultured on artificial media.

Peronosclerospora sacchari

Oomycota Peronosporaceae Not culturable
Classification

Kingdom Chromista, Phylum Oomycota, Class Peronosporomycetes, Order Peronosporales, Family Peronosporaceae. Obligate biotroph.

Description

Causal agent of maize downy mildew and sugarcane downy mildew. Produces evanescent sporangiophores with multiple branches bearing sporangia. Obligate parasite of grasses (maize, sorghum, sugarcane). Difficult to classify by standard techniques. P. sacchari and P. philippinensis exhibit identical phenotypes for 22 enzymes and probably represent a single species.

Isolation

Cannot be cultured on artificial media — obligate biotroph. Detection by DNA hybridisation in endosperm, pericarp, and pedicel tissues of infected maize seeds. Also detected by microscopic examination of sporangiophores and sporangia from infected leaf tissue.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on sporangiophore branching and sporangia size/shape (affected by temperature and host cultivar).
  • Molecular confirmation by DNA hybridisation using plasmid pCLY83 probes.
  • Isozyme analysis and aminopeptidase assays are used for genus-level discrimination.
  • ITS sequencing and sequence comparison with P. maydis, P. philippinensis, and P. sorghi are recommended.

Sclerospora graminicola

Oomycota Peronosporaceae Not culturable
Classification

Kingdom Chromista, Phylum Oomycota, Class Peronosporomycetes, Order Peronosporales, Family Peronosporaceae. Obligate biotroph.

Description

Causes downy mildew of pearl millet and other graminaceous hosts. Produces evanescent sporangiophores with multiple branches bearing sporangia — uniquely distinguishes Sclerospora graminicola from members of Peronosporaceae outside the genus Sclerospora. Differs from S. isellematis and S. secalina by having an oogonial wall with conspicuous ridges. Oospores are yellowish-brown, spherical, smooth, thick-walled (3 layers), 30–44 µm diameter (average ~32 µm). Predominantly heterothallic.

Isolation

Cannot be cultured on artificial media — obligate biotroph. Maintained on susceptible pearl millet cultivars. Detection by microscopic examination of surface washings for oospores. Shoot tip, hypocotyl, and inflorescence explants can be cultured on Murashige and Skoog tissue culture medium for maintenance.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on sporangiophore branching, oospore morphology, and oogonial wall ridges.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and sequence analysis.
  • The genus Sclerospora has been suggested to be separated into Fusclerospora and Peronosclerospora.

Sphacelotheca reiliana (Head smut)

Basidiomycota Microbotryaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Basidiomycota, Class Microbotryomycetes, Order Microbotryales, Family Microbotryaceae. Current name: Sporisorium reilianum (J.G. Kühn) Langdon & Full. Synonym: Sphacelotheca reiliana (Kühn) Clint.

Description

Causes head smut of sorghum, sudangrass, and corn. Sori form in the ovaries, infecting all spikelets in a panicle; sori are elongate-ellipsoidal, 5–8 mm. Teliospores are subglobose to globose, some slightly irregular, minutely echinulate. Teliospores of S. reiliana are distinguished from S. cruenta and S. sorghi by their conspicuous spiny walls and larger diameter.

Isolation

Isolate from smutted heads by collecting teliospores or plating infected tissue on PDA or MEA. The fungus is a semi-obligate pathogen but can be cultured on artificial media. Incubate at 25°C for 7–14 days. Teliospores can be germinated on water agar to produce basidia and sporidia.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on teliospore morphology (spiny walls, larger diameter) and sorus characteristics.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and LSU sequencing.
  • The taxonomy of Sphacelotheca and Sporisorium has been revised; S. reiliana is now classified as Sporisorium reilianum.

Ustilago maydis (Common smut)

Basidiomycota Ustilaginaceae
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Basidiomycota, Class Ustilaginomycetes, Order Ustilaginales, Family Ustilaginaceae. Current name: Mycosarcoma maydis (de Candolle) Brefeld. Synonym: Ustilago maydis (De Candolle) Corda.

Description

Semi-obligate plant pathogenic fungus. Causes common smut (corn smut) of maize (Zea mays). Produces large, tumour-like galls on all above-ground parts of the plant, initially silvery-white, turning black as teliospores mature. Teliospores are dark, spherical to irregular, and ornamented. The fungus is a basidiomycete with a complex life cycle involving uninuclate and probably diploid lines that produce tumours and teliospores.

Isolation

Isolate from smut galls by collecting teliospores or plating surface-sterilised gall tissue on PDA or MEA. Teliospores germinate on water agar to produce basidia and sporidia. The fungus can be cultured on standard mycological media. Incubate at 25–28°C for 3–7 days.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on gall formation on maize, teliospore morphology, and colony characteristics.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and LSU sequencing.
  • Mating and progeny isolation protocols are well established for this model organism.

Phragmidium acuminatum (Rust)

Basidiomycota Phragmidiaceae Not culturable
Classification

Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Basidiomycota, Class Pucciniomycetes, Order Pucciniales (rust fungi), Family Phragmidiaceae. Rust fungi are obligate plant pathogens with ~8,000 described species in 7 suborders.

Description

Phragmidium acuminatum is a rust fungus infecting Sanguisorba species. Produces characteristic rust pustules (uredinia and telia) on leaves and stems. Teliospores are multi-celled, dark, and thick-walled. Rust species are host-specific and cause disease on economically significant plants.

Isolation

Cannot be cultured on artificial media — obligate biotroph. Detection by direct microscopic examination of rust pustules and spores from infected plant tissue. Maintained on living host plants.

Identification
  • Morphological identification based on spore morphology (urediniospores, teliospores), pustule characteristics, and host specificity.
  • Molecular confirmation by ITS sequencing and LSU sequencing.
  • DNA barcode-based identification using the Rust HUBB system is available for Pucciniales.

5 Laboratory Diagnostic Workflow

Step-by-step diagnostic pathway from sample receipt through to reporting, applicable to all pathogens listed above.

Diagnostic workflow steps, actions and notes
Step Action Notes
1. Sample receipt Record sample type, origin, and symptoms Maintain chain of custody
2. Direct examination Microscopic examination of infected tissue For rusts, downy mildews, smuts — observe spores/pustules
3. Isolation Plate on general or selective media PDA for most fungi; selective media for Fusarium, Phytophthora, Pythium, Rhizoctonia
4. Purification Single-spore or hyphal tip transfer Essential for mixed infections
5. Morphological ID Colony characteristics, spore morphology Requires expertise; some species indistinguishable
6. Molecular ID ITS sequencing (universal barcode) Confirmatory; essential for species-level discrimination
7. Pathogenicity test Inoculation on susceptible host Koch’s postulates; confirms virulence
8. Reporting Species name, pathogenicity, quarantine status EPPO A1/A2 pests flagged
Notes on culture-independent pathogens: Spongospora subterranea, Synchytrium endobioticum, Peronosclerospora sacchari, Sclerospora graminicola, and Phragmidium acuminatum are obligate biotrophs and cannot be cultured on artificial media. Detection relies on direct microscopy, baiting, or molecular methods (PCR / sequencing).
Quarantine note: Synchytrium endobioticum (potato wart disease) is an EPPO A1 quarantine pest. Suspected detections must be reported immediately and confirmed by a designated reference laboratory using molecular and biological methods.
