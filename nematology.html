Nematodes of Quarantine & Regulatory Concern

Classification, extraction and identification notes for nematode species and groups detected in samples submitted to the station for testing.

2 Extraction Methods

Extraction method selection depends on the sample matrix (soil vs. plant tissue) and the nematode's biology — endoparasitic vs. ectoparasitic, cyst-forming vs. free-living.

From plant tissue

  • Baermann funnel / mist chamber — suitable for extracting Ditylenchus angustus from rice tillers and Aphelenchoides ritzemabosi from leaf tissues. Plant pieces are placed in water and nematodes migrate out over 24 hours.
  • Direct dissection — for Meloidogyne spp., root galls are dissected under a microscope to reveal adult females and egg masses.
  • Water soaking — Ditylenchus destructor and D. dipsaci can be detected by placing infested plant tissue (tubers, bulbs) in water.

From soil

  • Baermann pan / funnel — validated for extracting D. dipsaci from various soil types (sand, loam, silty clay, muck); also used for motile stages of Meloidogyne and Xiphinema.
  • Fenwick can / flotation — the standard method for recovering potato cyst nematodes (Globodera spp.) from soil. The Fenwick can is recommended for routine analysis as it recovers the highest number of cysts.
  • Sugar centrifugal flotation — used for extracting Aphelenchoides from soil and for separating Meloidogyne juveniles from soil samples.
  • USDA cyst extractor / elutriation — alternative acceptable methods for cyst extraction.

3 Identification Methods

Identification follows a morphological → molecular workflow, with molecular confirmation essential for species-level discrimination, especially for quarantine pests.

Sample receipt → Extraction → Morphology → Molecular confirmation → Reporting

3.1 Morphological identification

  • Ditylenchus angustus — distinguished by tail shape and size, relative stylet length, post-vulval sac, and number of cuticular lateral lines. Adult females are 800–1,200 µm long.
  • Ditylenchus destructor vs. D. dipsaci — morphologically very similar (same genus, similar size); identification relies on morphological methods first, often followed by molecular confirmation.
  • Globodera pallida / G. rostochiensis — cyst morphology and morphometrics of second-stage juveniles (J2), especially stylet length and shape of basal knobs, are used. However, morphological overlap requires molecular confirmation.
  • Meloidogyne chitwoodi / M. fallax — identification based on female perineal patterns and J2 morphometrics. These species are morphologically very close and often require molecular tools for reliable separation.
  • Aphelenchoides ritzemabosi — morphological identification alone is difficult; species-level discrimination requires molecular methods.

3.2 Molecular identification (key methods)

  • PCR-RFLP (ITS region) — used for distinguishing Ditylenchus species, Globodera species and Meloidogyne species.
  • Multiplex PCR — recommended for routine identification of G. pallida and G. rostochiensis using ITS primers (e.g. ITS5 / PITSp4 + PITSr3).
  • Real-time PCR — high-throughput diagnosis of PCN in soil samples; also validated for Globodera and Meloidogyne.
  • LAMP (loop-mediated isothermal amplification) — successfully used to differentiate M. chitwoodi from M. hapla and tropical root-knot nematodes.
  • SCAR markers — used for specific detection of D. dipsaci and M. incognita.
Note on generic records: samples logged only as "Nematodes", "PCN" or "Root knot nematodes" require follow-up to genus or species level before a phytosanitary decision can be made.

5 Practical Recommendations for These Samples

Soil samples labelled "PCN"

Extract cysts using a Fenwick can or sugar flotation, then apply multiplex PCR to differentiate G. pallida from G. rostochiensis.

Rice samples with D. angustus

Extract from tillers using a Baermann funnel; confirm with morphology (stylet, tail) and PCR-RFLP where required.

Irish potato with multiple species

Use separate strategies — cyst extraction for Globodera and Punctodera, Baermann for Ditylenchus, root dissection for Meloidogyne.

Generic "Nematodes" samples

Likely mixed populations; target the dominant life stage (motile vs. cyst) during extraction and confirm key genera molecularly.

Cocopeat and ornamentals

Xiphinema and Aphelenchoides are best recovered by Baermann funnel from soil/medium and plant tissue respectively; molecular confirmation is advised for species-level ID.
