Tomato brown rugose fruit virus (ToBRFV)

▼
Classification
Family
Virgaviridae
Genus
Tobamovirus
Genome
Positive-sense single-stranded RNA, ~6.4 kb, rod-shaped particles
Type Member
Tobacco mosaic virus (TMV)
Identification
Symptoms
Brown rugose (wrinkled) patches on tomato fruit, mosaic and mottling on leaves, fruit deformation, yellowing
Natural Hosts
Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum), pepper (Capsicum spp.)
Transmission
Mechanically (contact, tools, hands), seed-borne, not by insects
Tomato Pepper Capsicum
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-qPCR (TaqMan) — primary diagnostic method Target: Conserved region of the coat protein (CP) gene / movement protein gene Sample: Leaf tissue or seed RNA Extraction: Total RNA using commercial kits (e.g., Qiagen RNeasy) RT-qPCR Cycling: 50°C for 20 min (Reverse Transcription) 95°C for 5 min (RT inactivation) 40 cycles: 95°C for 15 sec → 60°C for 30 sec Primers/Probes: Multiple validated assays available (e.g., Menzel & Winter 2021; Rodríguez et al. 2019). TaqMan probes with FAM/BHQ1 labels. Alternative: Conventional RT-PCR for endpoint detection; amplicon sequencing for confirmation.
⚠️ ToBRFV can break Tm-2² resistance in tomato. Seed testing is critical for phytosanitary control. False negatives are possible with low seed titers — use of internal positive control (IPC) is recommended.
Useful Information
  • First reported in Jordan (2015) and Israel; now widespread in Europe, Americas, and Asia.
  • Extremely stable in the environment; survives on surfaces for long periods.
  • EPPO A2 quarantine pest status in many regions.

Tomato spotted wilt virus (TSWV)

▼
Classification
Family
Tospoviridae (formerly Bunyaviridae)
Genus
Orthotospovirus
Genome
Negative/ambisense single-stranded RNA, tripartite (S, M, L segments), enveloped spherical particles (80–120 nm)
Type Species
Tomato spotted wilt virus
Identification
Symptoms
Chlorotic/necrotic ringspots, bronzing, wilting, stunting; highly variable depending on host and stage
Natural Hosts
Very broad — tomato, pepper, chrysanthemum, gypsophila, eustoma, limonium, alstroemeria, aster, many ornamentals and vegetables
Transmission
Thrips (vector-specific); Frankliniella occidentalis is the main vector
Tomato Pepper Chrysanthemum Gypsophila Eustoma Limonium Alstroemeria Aster Phalaenopsis Dracaena
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-PCR (endpoint) — standard diagnostic method Target: Nucleocapsid (N) gene on the S RNA segment Sample: Leaf tissue (symptomatic) RNA Extraction: Total RNA (TRIzol or column-based) One-Step RT-PCR: cDNA synthesis: 50°C for 30 min Initial denaturation: 94°C for 2 min 35 cycles: 94°C for 30 sec → 55°C for 30 sec → 72°C for 45 sec Final extension: 72°C for 5 min Expected amplicon: ~450 bp (degenerate primers) or ~819 bp (N-gene specific primers) Confirmation: Restriction enzyme digestion (DraI/HindIII) to differentiate TSWV from INSV, or amplicon sequencing. Alternative: DAS-ELISA for high-throughput screening; RT-qPCR for increased sensitivity.
⚠️ TSWV is the type species of the genus and has a very broad host range. Mixed infections with INSV are common in ornamentals; differentiation requires either restriction digestion or specific primers.
Useful Information
  • One of the most economically important plant viruses worldwide.
  • Thrips acquire the virus as larvae and transmit persistently (propagative).
  • Weed reservoirs are important in epidemiology.
  • Management relies on vector control, resistant varieties, and clean planting material.

Impatiens necrotic spot virus (INSV)

▼
Classification
Family
Tospoviridae
Genus
Orthotospovirus
Genome
Tripartite ambisense RNA, enveloped spherical particles
Identification
Symptoms
Necrotic spots, ringspots, stem necrosis, wilting; similar to TSWV but often more necrotic
Natural Hosts
Impatiens, chrysanthemum, eustoma, tomato, basil, many ornamentals
Transmission
Thrips (Frankliniella occidentalis, F. fusca)
Impatiens Chrysanthemum Eustoma Tomato
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-PCR (endpoint) Target: Nucleocapsid (N) gene Sample: Symptomatic leaf tissue RNA Extraction: Total RNA RT-PCR Cycling: 50°C for 30 min (RT) 94°C for 2 min (denaturation) 35 cycles: 94°C for 30 sec → 55°C for 30 sec → 72°C for 45 sec 72°C for 5 min Expected amplicon: ~450 bp (degenerate tospovirus primers) or specific INSV primers targeting N, NSm, or RdRp genes (fragments of 883 bp, 712 bp, 1201 bp reported) Alternative: Real-time RT-qPCR with TaqMan probes (detection limit ~100 copies/µl); immunostrips for rapid field screening.
Useful Information
  • Formerly known as "Impatiens necrotic spot tospovirus" — now classified under Orthotospovirus impatiensnecromaculae.
  • Very problematic in ornamental greenhouses where thrips are difficult to control.
  • Often occurs in mixed infections with TSWV.

Chrysanthemum stem necrosis virus (CSNV)

▼
Classification
Family
Tospoviridae (formerly Bunyaviridae)
Genus
Orthotospovirus
Genome
Tripartite ambisense RNA
Identification
Symptoms
Stem necrosis (often black streaks), leaf necrosis, wilting, stunting
Natural Hosts
Chrysanthemum, sweet pepper, tomato
Transmission
Thrips (Frankliniella occidentalis)
Chrysanthemum Pepper
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-PCR Target: Nucleocapsid protein (N) gene region Sample: Symptomatic stem or leaf tissue RNA Extraction: Total RNA RT-PCR: Standard one-step or two-step protocol with CSNV-specific primers (N gene region). Expected amplicon: typically 300–500 bp (N gene based). Alternative: Real-time RT-PCR for increased sensitivity and quantification.
Useful Information
  • First described in Japan (2006) in chrysanthemum.
  • Closely related to TSWV but distinct by serology and sequence (N protein identity <90%).
  • Can also infect sweet pepper (Capsicum annuum) naturally.
  • Management focuses on thrips control and use of virus-free cuttings.

Chrysanthemum stunt viroid (CSVd)

▼
Classification
Family
Pospiviroidae
Genus
Pospiviroid
Genome
Circular, single-stranded RNA (~354–356 nt), non-coding
Type Species
Potato spindle tuber viroid (PSTVd)
Identification
Symptoms
Stunting, delayed flowering, small flowers, chlorotic mottling, leaf distortion; may be symptomless in some cultivars
Natural Hosts
Chrysanthemum (Dendranthema morifolium), various ornamentals
Transmission
Mechanically (sap, tools), vegetative propagation
Chrysanthemum
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-PCR (endpoint) or RT-qPCR Target: Conserved regions of the CSVd genome Sample: Leaf or stem tissue (young tissue preferred) RNA Extraction: Total RNA (silica-based or TRIzol) One-Step RT-PCR: 50°C for 30 min 95°C for 2 min 35 cycles: 94°C for 30 sec → 55–60°C for 30 sec → 72°C for 30 sec 72°C for 5 min Expected amplicon: ~300–400 bp depending on primer pair (e.g., CSVd-ZF/ZR primers) Alternative: Tissue blot hybridization; dot-blot; RT-qPCR (sensitivity ~100,000× higher than PAGE)
⚠️ CSVd can be present in symptomless plants. Routine indexing of mother stock is essential. Direct RT-PCR from minimal tissue (microtissue direct RT-PCR) is useful for screening small plantlets.
Useful Information
  • EPPO A2 quarantine pest.
  • Very stable RNA — resistant to heat and ribonucleases.
  • Elimination through meristem tip culture combined with thermotherapy.

Cucumber mosaic virus (CMV)

▼
Classification
Family
Bromoviridae
Genus
Cucumovirus
Genome
Positive-sense single-stranded RNA, tripartite (RNA1, RNA2, RNA3), icosahedral particles (~29 nm)
Identification
Symptoms
Mosaic, mottling, vein clearing, stunting, leaf distortion; variable depending on host
Natural Hosts
Very broad — cucumber, tomato, pepper, strawberry, raspberry, blueberry, many ornamentals and weeds
Transmission
Aphids (non-persistent, >80 species), seed-borne in some hosts
Cucumber Tomato Pepper Strawberry Raspberry Blueberry Veronica
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-PCR Target: Coat protein (CP) gene or RNA-1 segment Sample: Leaf tissue RNA Extraction: Total RNA (crude extracts also work) RT-PCR: One-step or two-step RT-PCR with CMV-specific primers. Expected amplicon: ~500–600 bp (CP gene); ~513 bp (RdRp region). Subgroup Differentiation: Restriction digestion with PvuII and VspI distinguishes CMV subgroups I and II. Alternative: DAS-ELISA; multiplex RT-PCR for simultaneous detection of multiple viruses.
Useful Information
  • One of the most widespread plant viruses worldwide.
  • Transmitted very efficiently by aphids in a non-persistent manner.
  • Satellite RNAs (satRNA) can modulate symptom severity.

Maize chlorotic mottle virus (MCMV)

▼
Classification
Family
Tombusviridae
Genus
Machlomovirus
Genome
Positive-sense single-stranded RNA, icosahedral particles (~30 nm)
Identification
Symptoms
Chlorotic mottling, stunting, leaf yellowing; often asymptomatic in some genotypes
Natural Hosts
Maize, sweet corn, baby corn
Transmission
Thrips (maize thrips), seed-borne, mechanically
Maize Sweet Corn Baby Corn
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-qPCR (TaqMan) and RT-PCR Target: Coat protein (CP) gene Sample: Leaf tissue, seed RNA Extraction: Total RNA (TRIzol or column-based) RT-qPCR Cycling: 50°C for 20 min (RT) 95°C for 5 min 40 cycles: 95°C for 15 sec → 60°C for 30 sec Alternative: DAS-ELISA for high-throughput seed testing; conventional RT-PCR (expected amplicon ~1.4 kb with degenerate tenuivirus primers; specific MCMV primers yield smaller products).
⚠️ MCMV is a key component of Maize Lethal Necrosis (MLN) when co-infecting with Sugarcane mosaic virus (SCMV) or other potyviruses. Synergistic interaction causes severe disease and yield loss.
Useful Information
  • Major threat to maize production in East Africa, South America, and Asia.
  • Seed transmission is an important pathway for long-distance spread.
  • Management includes vector control, resistant varieties, and clean seed.

Sugarcane mosaic virus (SCMV)

▼
Classification
Family
Potyviridae
Genus
Potyvirus
Genome
Positive-sense single-stranded RNA, monopartite, filamentous particles (~750 nm)
Identification
Symptoms
Mosaic, mottling, chlorotic streaks, stunting
Natural Hosts
Sugarcane, maize, sorghum, and other grasses
Transmission
Aphids (non-persistent), mechanically
Sugarcane Maize Sweet Corn
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-PCR (endpoint) Target: Coat protein (CP) gene or NIb gene Sample: Leaf tissue RNA Extraction: Total RNA RT-PCR: One-step RT-PCR with SCMV-specific primers.   • Co-infection with MCMV causes Maize Lethal Necrosis (MLN).
  • Strain differentiation (A, B, D, E) is based on molecular and biological properties.

Potato spindle tuber viroid (PSTVd)

▼
Classification
Family
Pospiviroidae
Genus
Pospiviroid
Genome
Circular, single-stranded RNA (341–364 nt), non-coding
Type Species
Potato spindle tuber viroid
Identification
Symptoms
Spindle-shaped tubers, stunting, leaf rolling, yield reduction; symptomless in many ornamentals
Natural Hosts
Potato, tomato, pepper, ornamental Solanaceae (Solanum jasminoides, Brugmansia, Dahlia)
Transmission
Mechanically (sap, tools), vegetative propagation, true seed (rare)
Potato Tomato Pepper Dahlia
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-PCR (endpoint) and real-time RT-PCR Target: Conserved central region of the PSTVd genome Sample: Leaf tissue, tuber tissue RNA Extraction: Total RNA (silica-based or TRIzol) Conventional RT-PCR (Verhoeven et al. 2004): 50°C for 30 min (RT) 95°C for 2 min 35 cycles: 94°C for 30 sec → 55°C for 30 sec → 72°C for 30 sec 72°C for 5 min Expected amplicon: ~270–360 bp depending on primer pair (e.g., 271 bp for PSTVd with multiplex primers) Real-time RT-PCR: GenPospi assay (Botermans et al. 2013) for broad pospiviroid detection; specific PSTVd probes for confirmation. Alternative: DIG-labeled cRNA probe hybridization; RT-PCR followed by sequencing for definitive identification.
⚠️ PSTVd is a quarantine pest in many countries (EPPO A2). It can be symptomless in ornamental hosts, making routine testing of imported plant material essential. Sequencing of amplicons is recommended for confirmation due to cross-reactivity with other pospiviroids.
Useful Information
  • The first viroid discovered (1971).
  • Very stable and highly contagious — strict sanitation is critical.
  • Elimination through meristem culture and thermotherapy.

Ralstonia solanacearum (species complex)

▼
Classification
Domain
Bacteria
Phylum
Pseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
Class
Betaproteobacteria
Order
Burkholderiales
Family
Burkholderiaceae
Genus
Ralstonia
Species Complex
R. solanacearum sensu lato (includes R. pseudosolanacearum, R. syzygii)
Identification
Symptoms
Wilting, vascular browning, bacterial ooze, stunting; "brown rot" in potato, "Moko disease" in banana, "Southern wilt" in various crops
Natural Hosts
Very broad — potato, tomato, pepper, banana, pelargonium, geranium, and many others
Transmission
Soil-borne, water-borne, infected planting material, tools
Potato Tomato Pepper Banana Pelargonium Geranium
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: PCR (endpoint) — because it is a bacterium Target: Species-specific primer pairs (e.g., 759/760 for RSSC; phylotype-specific multiplex PCR) Sample: Vascular tissue, stem sections, soil, water DNA Extraction: CTAB method or commercial bacterial DNA kits PCR Cycling (Opina et al. 1997 primers 759/760): 95°C for 1 min 30 cycles: 95°C for 15 sec → annealing temp for 15 sec → 72°C for 10 sec 72°C for 5 min Expected amplicon: ~280–282 bp (RSSC-specific) Multiplex Phylotype PCR: Differentiates phylotypes I, II, III, IV based on specific primer sets. Alternative: Isolation on selective media (e.g., TTC medium), followed by PCR confirmation; real-time PCR for quantification.
⚠️ Ralstonia solanacearum is a quarantine pathogen in many countries. The species complex is taxonomically complex — phylotype and sequevar identification is important for epidemiological tracking and regulatory decisions.
Useful Information
  • One of the most destructive bacterial plant pathogens worldwide.
  • Survives in soil for years; rotation and sanitation are critical.
  • Race 3 biovar 2 is a quarantine pathogen in Europe and North America.

Xylella fastidiosa

▼
Classification
Domain
Bacteria
Phylum
Pseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
Class
Gammaproteobacteria
Order
Lysobacterales (Xanthomonadales)
Family
Lysobacteraceae (Xanthomonadaceae)
Genus
Xylella
Subspecies
fastidiosa, multiplex, pauca, sandyi, tashke
Identification
Symptoms
Leaf scorch, marginal necrosis, stunting, dieback; "Pierce's disease" in grape, "citrus variegated chlorosis", "coffee leaf scorch"
Natural Hosts
Very broad — grape, citrus, coffee, olive, almond, ornamental plants (Eustoma, Solidago, Nepenthes, Lavandula, etc.)
Transmission
Xylem-feeding insects (sharpshooters, spittlebugs, cicadellids)
Grape Citrus Coffee Olive Eustoma Lavandula Nepenthes
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: PCR (endpoint) and real-time PCR (qPCR) Target: 16S rRNA gene, mutS gene, or subspecies-specific genomic regions Sample: Petiole tissue (preferred), stem tissue DNA Extraction: CTAB method with additional purification steps (plant inhibitors are common) Conventional PCR: Universal primers for Xylella spp.; subspecies-specific primers for typing. Real-time PCR (qPCR): Subspecies-specific TaqMan assays; multiplex tetraplex qPCR for simultaneous detection and subspecies identification. Multilocus Sequence Typing (MLST): For epidemiological tracking. Alternative: Isolation on periwinkle wilt (PW) medium — slow and difficult; serological methods (ELISA) less sensitive.
⚠️ Xylella fastidiosa is a quarantine pathogen of major concern (EU priority pest). Detection can be challenging due to low and uneven bacterial titers in plant tissues — sampling multiple petioles from different parts of the plant is recommended.
Useful Information
  • Cause of Pierce's disease of grapevine and citrus variegated chlorosis.
  • The pauca subspecies is responsible for the olive quick decline syndrome (OQDS) in Italy.
  • Management focuses on vector control, removal of infected plants, and quarantine measures.

Banana bunchy top virus (BBTV)

▼
Classification
Family
Nanoviridae
Genus
Babuvirus
Genome
Circular single-stranded DNA, multipartite (6 components), icosahedral particles (~18–20 nm)
Identification
Symptoms
"Bunchy top" — dark green streaks on petioles, leaves become narrow and upright, stunted growth, no fruit or small fruit
Natural Hosts
Banana, plantain (Musa spp.)
Transmission
Banana aphid (Pentalonia nigronervosa) — persistent, circulative; vegetative propagation
Banana Plantain
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: PCR and real-time PCR (qPCR) — because BBTV has a DNA genome Target: Coat protein (CP) gene or other core component genes Sample: Leaf tissue (young, symptomatic), pseudostem DNA Extraction: CTAB method or commercial plant DNA kits Conventional PCR: BBTV-specific primers targeting CP gene; expected amplicon ~500 bp. Real-time PCR (TaqMan): Highly sensitive detection in plants and aphids; detection limit ~1.0 ng–1.0 mg of infected leaf tissue. Multiplexing: Can be multiplexed with BSV (Banana streak virus) and CMV for simultaneous detection. Alternative: Rolling circle amplification (RCA) for whole-genome characterization; ELISA less reliable for this virus.
Useful Information
  • One of the most serious viral diseases of banana worldwide.
  • Threatens production in Africa, Asia, and the Pacific.
  • Management relies on aphid control, eradication of infected plants, and use of virus-free planting material.

Avsunviroid

African cassava mosaic virus (ACMV)

▼
Classification
Family
Geminiviridae
Genus
Begomovirus
Genome
Circular single-stranded DNA, bipartite (DNA-A and DNA-B), twinned icosahedral particles (~22 × 38 nm)
Identification
Symptoms
Mosaic, mottling, leaf distortion, stunting, reduced tuber yield
Natural Hosts
Cassava (Manihot esculenta)
Transmission
Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) — persistent, circulative; vegetative propagation
Cassava
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: PCR (endpoint) and real-time PCR (qPCR) Target: DNA-A and DNA-B genomic components; conserved regions of the coat protein (AV1) gene Sample: Leaf tissue (symptomatic) DNA Extraction: CTAB method Conventional PCR: Begomovirus universal primers (e.g., AV494/CoPR) targeting partial AV1 gene; expected amplicon ~550 bp. East African cassava mosaic virus (EACMV)

▼
Classification
Family
Geminiviridae
Genus
Begomovirus
Genome
Circular single-stranded DNA, bipartite (DNA-A and DNA-B)
Identification
Symptoms
Severe mosaic, leaf distortion, stunting; often more severe than ACMV alone
Natural Hosts
Cassava
Transmission
Whitefly, vegetative propagation
Cassava
Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: PCR and real-time PCR (qPCR) Target: DNA-A and DNA-B components; species-specific primers Sample: Leaf tissue DNA Extraction: CTAB method Conventional PCR: EACMV-specific primers targeting DNA-A; can be multiplexed with ACMV-specific primers for simultaneous detection. Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: PCR / RT-PCR depending on virus type RoLCuV (DNA virus): PCR with begomovirus universal primers (AV494/CoPR); expected amplicon ~550 bp. RCA for full genome. Rose mosaic complex (RNA viruses): RT-PCR for PNRSV (CP gene, ~400 bp) and ApMV (CP gene, ~900 bp). Multiplex RT-PCR can detect both simultaneously. Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: RT-PCR (endpoint) and multiplex IC-RT-PCR Target: Coat protein (CP) gene; movement protein gene Sample: Leaf tissue, seed RNA Extraction: Total RNA Multiplex IC-RT-PCR: Simultaneous detection and differentiation of TMV and ToMV in a single reaction.   • TMV was the first virus ever discovered (1892 by Ivanovsky).
  • Extremely stable — can survive in plant debris and on surfaces for years.
  • ToMV resistance genes (Tm-1, Tm-2, Tm-2²) are widely used in tomato breeding.

Procedure / Protocol for Identification
Method: PCR (endpoint) and nested PCR Target: 16S rRNA gene; 16S–23S intergenic spacer region Sample: Leaf tissue (midribs preferred), stem tissue DNA Extraction: CTAB method with phytoplasma-specific enrichment (phytoplasmas are low-titer and unevenly distributed) Direct PCR: Universal primers P1/P7 (amplify ~1.8 kb of 16S rRNA and ITS region) Cycling: 94°C for 2 min; 35 cycles: 94°C for 30 sec → 55°C for 30 sec → 72°C for 90 sec; 72°C for 10 min Nested PCR: Primer pair R16F2n/R16R2 (amplifies ~1.2 kb internal fragment of 16S rRNA gene) Cycling: 94°C for 2 min; 35 cycles: 94°C for 30 sec → 55°C for 30 sec → 72°C for 60 sec; 72°C for 10 min Group Identification: RFLP analysis of nested PCR products or sequencing; qPCR for quantification.   • The 16SrI group contains many economically important strains.
  • Management focuses on vector control, removal of infected plants, and use of clean propagation material.
